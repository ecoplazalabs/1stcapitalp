## What happened

UK gilt yields fell substantially over the week, marking the largest weekly decline since 2024. The rally was driven by renewed fiscal discipline messaging and a pullback from market bets on higher Bank of England interest rates.

## Key figures

- **Timeframe**: Largest weekly yield drop since 2024
- **Primary driver 1**: Fiscal rules commitment from Burnham
- **Primary driver 2**: Reduction in BoE rate rise expectations

## Why it matters for the lower middle-market

For lower middle-market operators and sponsors, gilts serve as a barometer for the broader cost of capital. A sustained decline in gilt yields typically filters through to improved availability and pricing of debt financing for acquisitions and leverage-supported buyouts. The shift in rate expectations particularly affects cross-border M&A transactions where UK-domiciled or UK-listed targets are refinanced post-deal, making borrowing costs more predictable. Additionally, sponsors reviewing portfolio company valuations will benefit from lower discount rates applied to earnings forecasts.
