## What happened

Roger Altman of Evercore has identified an emerging vulnerability in oil markets that poses risks to broader financial stability. He contends that sustained crude price appreciation could precipitate both near-term market disruption and inflationary pressures across the economy.

## Key figures

- **Critical oil price level**: $150 per barrel or higher identified as a potential trigger for market instability
- **Inflation risk context**: described as "the second big inflation shock of this decade after COVID"

## Why it matters for the lower middle-market

For lower middle-market operators and their sponsors, an oil-driven inflation shock would compress margins across supply chains and increase working capital requirements. Cross-border M&A activity could face headwinds if equity markets destabilise in response to commodity-driven inflation expectations. Refinancing and growth capital decisions should factor in the asymmetric risk of energy-linked inflation, particularly for businesses with embedded commodity exposure or dollar-denominated debt obligations.

> "A sharp rise in crude could destabilise markets before triggering the second big inflation shock of this decade after COVID," according to Altman's CNBC remarks on Monday.
