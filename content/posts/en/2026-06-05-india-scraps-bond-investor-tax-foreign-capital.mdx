## What happened

India has scrapped capital gains tax on overseas bond investors as part of a package of measures designed to attract foreign capital inflows. The policy shift forms part of a wider effort to strengthen the rupee and enhance the nation's fixed income markets.

## Key figures

- **Tax measure**: Capital gains tax eliminated for foreign investors in government bonds
- **Stated objective**: Boost capital inflows and shore up the rupee

## Why it matters for the lower middle-market

For lower middle-market sponsors and cross-border investors, India's tax removal on bond investments signals a meaningful shift in emerging market accessibility. Enhanced foreign investor participation in Indian government bonds typically correlates with improved rupee stability and lower borrowing costs across asset classes, which can benefit firms with Indian operations or exposure. The initiative underscores how emerging market governments compete for capital—a dynamic relevant to lower middle-market operators evaluating geographic expansion or refinancing strategies in Asia.
