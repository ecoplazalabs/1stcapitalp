## What happened

The 2-year U.S. Treasury yield, the most sensitive indicator of near-term Fed policy expectations, rose almost three basis points following what markets interpreted as a hawkish start to the new Federal Reserve chair's tenure under Warsh.

## Key figures

- **2-year Treasury yield movement**: rose almost three basis points
- **Yield sensitivity**: most reactive to changes in fed funds rate expectations

## Why it matters for the lower middle-market

Movements in the 2-year Treasury yield directly influence borrowing costs for acquisition finance, asset-based lending, and working capital facilities that lower middle-market sponsors and operators rely upon. A rise in short-duration yields typically precedes broader repricing across the credit curve, affecting both debt refinancing timelines and the return hurdle rates applied to new acquisitions. Market perception of Fed policy stance under new leadership can shift investor appetite for risk assets and credit spreads, which in turn impacts availability and pricing of growth capital for sub-$100M EBITDA targets.
