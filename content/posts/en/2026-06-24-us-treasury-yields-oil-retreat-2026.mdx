## What happened

The yield on the 10-year US Treasury note, a primary benchmark for government and corporate borrowing, fell by over 1 basis point to 4.479% (CNBC World, 24 June 2026). This decline coincided with oil prices retreating to pre-war levels.

## Key figures

- **10-year US Treasury yield**: 4.479%
- **Yield movement**: Down over 1 basis point

## Why it matters for the lower middle-market

Treasury yields serve as the foundation for corporate financing curves. A decline in the 10-year rate typically signals easier conditions for refinancing and new debt issuance, particularly relevant for sponsors and operators seeking acquisition financing or debt restructuring. Lower yields may also compress spreads on investment-grade credit, affecting the relative attractiveness of equity-like returns for institutional investors evaluating lower middle-market acquisitions. Energy sector weakness, reflected in oil prices, may create both headwinds for portfolio companies with energy exposure and tailwinds for counterparties seeking cheaper hedging costs.
