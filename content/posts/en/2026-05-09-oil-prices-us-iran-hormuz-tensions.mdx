## What happened

The U.S. and Iran exchanged fire in the Strait of Hormuz, with reports of U.S. engagement on Iranian tankers and missile attacks on the UAE. The incident has raised concerns about the sustainability of the existing ceasefire between the two nations.

## Key figures

- **Location**: Strait of Hormuz
- **Parties involved**: U.S., Iran, UAE
- **Market impact**: Oil prices edged higher following the incident
- **Risk status**: Fragile ceasefire described as "unraveling"

## Why it matters for the lower middle-market

For lower middle-market operators with exposure to energy-dependent supply chains or commodity pricing, escalating geopolitical tensions in the Strait of Hormuz represent a material risk to cost structures and working capital management. The volatility in crude pricing directly affects input costs across chemicals, petrochemicals, and downstream manufacturing sectors. Private equity sponsors should factor renewed Middle East geopolitical risk into portfolio company guidance and hedging strategies, particularly for businesses reliant on stable energy costs or exposed to regional trade flows.
