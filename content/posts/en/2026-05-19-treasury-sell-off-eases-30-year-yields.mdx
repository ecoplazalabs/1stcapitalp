## What happened

A period of intensity in U.S. Treasury markets has eased, though longer-duration yields remain elevated. Traders are monitoring the potential for 30-year yields to reach levels not witnessed since 1999, reflecting broader volatility in fixed income markets driven by inflation dynamics and anticipated central bank actions.

## Key figures

- **30-year yield target:** Highest levels since 1999
- **Market condition:** Treasury sell-off has eased from prior intensity

## Why it matters for the lower middle-market

Elevated and volatile Treasury yields directly affect acquisition financing costs and refinancing terms for lower middle-market operators and sponsors. Rising long-duration yields increase the cost of capital for leveraged transactions and dividend recaps. Cross-border M&A counterparties may reassess deal economics if U.S. borrowing costs remain elevated, potentially compressing valuations and narrowing the pool of financeable opportunities in the $10M–$100M revenue segment.
